2. **Willis et al. (2019)** "The Effects of Exercise Session Timing on Weight Loss and Components of Energy Balance" -- *International Journal of Obesity*
   - Retrospective analysis of MIDWEST Exercise Trial 2. Morning exercisers (before noon) lost -7.2% body weight vs -2.1% for late-day exercisers. Morning group also had higher total daily physical activity.

3. **Arciero et al. (2022)** "Morning Exercise Reduces Abdominal Fat and Blood Pressure in Women; Evening Exercise Increases Muscular Performance in Women and Reduces Blood Pressure in Men" -- *Frontiers in Physiology*

4. **Schumacher et al. (2020)** "Consistent Morning Exercise May Be Beneficial for Individuals With Obesity" -- *Exercise and Sport Sciences Reviews*

## Iteration 2 -- Muscle Performance & Cell Biology

**Strategy:** Search for "exercise time of day muscle strength performance" to find primary studies behind the meta-analyses already captured.

**Papers Found:**
1. **Kuusmaa et al. (2016)** "Effects of Morning vs. Evening Combined Strength and Endurance Training on Physical Performance, Muscle Hypertrophy, and Hormonal Responses" -- *Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism*
   - 24-week RCT, untrained men. PM training group showed superior hypertrophy during weeks 13-24 (late adaptation phase). No difference in weeks 1-12. Neither training time altered resting testosterone or cortisol ratios.

2. **Douglas et al. (2021)** "Time of Day and Muscle Strength: A Circadian Output?" -- *Physiology*
   - Review of physiological mechanisms. Maximum isometric strength 4-13% higher in late afternoon vs early morning. Driven by core body temperature rhythm, motor unit recruitment patterns, and calcium handling kinetics.

3. **Ezagouri et al. (2019)** "Physiological and Molecular Dissection of Daily Variance in Exercise Capacity" -- *Cell Metabolism*
   - Mouse + human study. Exercise timing resets the skeletal muscle peripheral clock. ZMP (a cell-autonomous metabolite) is 50% higher in the active phase. Exercise capacity varies with time of day through circadian metabolite oscillations.

## Iteration 3 -- Cardiovascular Health (Blood Pressure Focus)

**Strategy:** Search for "exercise timing blood pressure hypertension" to fill the CV Health gap beyond the existing meta-analysis.

**Papers Found:**
1. **Brito et al. (2019)** "Morning Versus Evening Aerobic Training Effects on Blood Pressure in Treated Hypertension" -- *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*
   - 10-week RCT, treated hypertensives. Evening training produced significantly greater 24-hour ambulatory BP reductions compared to morning training. Particularly pronounced for nocturnal dipping.

2. **Brito et al. (2022)** "Exercise Training Timing and Its Impact on Cardiovascular Health" -- *Comprehensive Physiology*
   - Review integrating multiple studies. Evening exercise associated with ~10mmHg greater systolic BP reduction in hypertensive populations. Proposed mechanism: evening exercise amplifies the natural nocturnal BP dip rather than fighting the morning cortisol surge.

## Iteration 5 -- Sleep Quality

**Strategy:** Search for "late evening exercise sleep quality disruption" to address a common concern and fill the Sleep Quality gap.

**Papers Found:**
1. **Kim et al. (2023)** "Effects of Exercise Timing on Sleep Architecture and Nocturnal Blood Pressure" -- *Physical Activity and Nutrition*
   - Crossover study, healthy adults. Neither morning nor evening moderate-intensity exercise significantly altered sleep architecture (onset latency, total sleep time, efficiency, REM proportion). Both improved subjective sleep quality vs no-exercise control.

2. **Yue et al. (2022)** "Effects of Evening Exercise on Sleep Quality: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis" -- *Nature and Science of Sleep*
   - Meta-analysis of 23 studies. Evening exercise of any intensity (moderate or vigorous) does not disrupt objective sleep measures when ending >=1h before bedtime. Challenges the traditional recommendation to avoid evening exercise.

3. **Goldberg et al. (2024)** "Is Evening Exercise Really Bad for Sleep?" -- *Journal of Sleep Research*
   - 4-condition crossover: AM moderate, PM moderate, PM vigorous, control. Intensity and proximity to bedtime matter far more than absolute clock time. Vigorous exercise ending <1h before bed impaired sleep onset latency by ~12min; moderate PM exercise had no effect.

**Coverage Assessment:**

**Decision:** KEPT. Coverage 6/8 -> 7/8 (+12.5%). Strong consensus across these three papers: evening exercise does not harm sleep under normal conditions. This overturns conventional wisdom and is one of the more clinically useful findings.
